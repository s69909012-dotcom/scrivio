Quick Answer

What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are tooth-replacement systems that use an implant fixture placed in the jawbone to support a replacement tooth. The complete restoration commonly includes an implant fixture, an abutment and a crown.

Understanding Implants

What Is a Dental Implant?

A dental implant is an artificial tooth-root system placed in the jawbone to support a replacement tooth.

3 Parts

Implant Fixture

The part placed in the jawbone that provides support for the replacement tooth.

Abutment

The connector between the implant fixture and the crown.

Crown

The visible replacement tooth attached to the abutment.

Implant Component

What Is the Implant Screw?

The implant screw, also called the implant fixture, is the part placed inside the jawbone. It acts as an artificial tooth root and provides support for the replacement tooth.

Treatment Journey

How Dental Implant Treatment Works

The exact treatment sequence can vary depending on the condition of the mouth and the planned restoration.

01

Assessment & Planning

The teeth, gums, bone support and relevant medical factors are assessed to plan an appropriate approach.

02

Implant Placement

The implant fixture is placed in the jawbone as part of the planned procedure.

03

Healing & Integration

A healing period allows the surrounding bone to integrate with the implant surface.

04

Restoration

When appropriate, the abutment and replacement tooth are fitted as part of the restorative stage.

Before Treatment

What to Consider Before Choosing Dental Implants

Several factors can affect whether dental implants are appropriate and how treatment may be planned.

Oral Health

The condition of the mouth, teeth and gums can influence treatment planning.

Jawbone Condition

Available bone support is considered when deciding whether and how an implant can be placed.

Healing

Healing and integration can affect the timing and progression of treatment.

Additional Treatment

Some people may need additional procedures before or during implant treatment, depending on their assessment.

Individual Treatment Plan

The treatment approach is planned according to the person's oral condition, needs and clinical assessment.

Suitability

Is Dental Implant Treatment Suitable for Everyone?

Not necessarily. Dental implants may not be appropriate in every situation. Suitability depends on factors such as oral health, gum condition, available jawbone support, healing considerations and relevant medical history.

A dentist needs to assess the individual situation before determining whether implant treatment is appropriate and whether any additional treatment may be needed.

Before You Choose

What to Keep in Mind

Dental implants are not suitable for everyone. Treatment depends on factors such as bone support, gum health, oral hygiene, healing considerations and relevant medical history.

A dentist can determine whether an implant is appropriate after an examination and, when needed, appropriate imaging and treatment planning.

The expected treatment timeline, restoration and maintenance requirements can also vary from person to person.

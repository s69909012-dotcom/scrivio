Dental Implants: Understanding Your Tooth Replacement Options

Missing teeth can affect chewing, comfort, and confidence. Learn how dental implants work, what the treatment involves, and what to consider before exploring your options.

Check Treatment Options →

What is a dental implant?

A dental implant is a small titanium component placed in the jawbone to support a replacement tooth. Once the implant has healed sufficiently, an abutment and restoration such as a crown may be fitted as part of the planned treatment.

Understanding Dental Implants

A Tooth Replacement Option Designed Around Your Case

Dental implants are one option for replacing missing teeth. Unlike a removable denture, an implant is placed into the jawbone and can provide support for a replacement tooth.

Treatment can vary depending on the number of missing teeth, their location, the condition of the gums and jawbone, and your overall oral health.

A professional dental assessment is therefore an important part of deciding whether implants may be suitable for you.

Treatment Process

What Happens During Dental Implant Treatment?

Dental implant treatment is usually completed through several stages. The exact process and timing can vary depending on the individual's situation.

01

Assessment & Planning

The dentist evaluates the missing tooth area, gums, jawbone, and overall oral condition. Imaging or other assessments may be used to understand the area and plan the treatment.

02

Implant Placement

The implant is placed into the jawbone at the planned location. The exact approach depends on the individual's treatment plan and clinical condition.

03

Healing & Integration

After placement, the implant is given time to heal and integrate with the surrounding bone. The length of this stage can vary between individuals.

04

Restoration

Once the implant has healed sufficiently, an abutment and replacement tooth such as a crown can be fitted as part of the planned restoration.

Before Treatment

What to Consider Before Choosing Dental Implants

Dental implants can be one option for replacing missing teeth, but treatment planning depends on several individual factors.

Oral Health

The condition of your gums, remaining teeth, and overall oral health may need to be assessed before implant treatment.

Jawbone Condition

The amount and condition of available jawbone can be an important part of implant planning.

Healing

After implant placement, the surrounding bone needs time to heal and integrate with the implant.

Additional Treatment

Some people may require other dental treatment before or alongside implant treatment, depending on their condition.

Individual Treatment Plan

The number of missing teeth, their location, and your overall dental condition can all influence the treatment approach.

Professional Assessment

A dental professional can assess your condition and discuss which tooth-replacement options may be appropriate for you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Dental Implant Questions

Are dental implants painful?

Implant placement is performed by a dental professional using appropriate anaesthesia. Some discomfort or swelling can occur afterwards, but the experience varies between individuals.

How long does dental implant treatment take?

Treatment time varies depending on the individual's oral health, bone condition, treatment plan, and healing response.

How long can a dental implant last?

Implant longevity can vary based on oral hygiene, professional maintenance, general health, and other individual factors.

Can everyone get dental implants?

Not everyone will be an immediate candidate. A dental professional needs to assess the gums, jawbone, remaining teeth, and overall oral condition before recommending a treatment approach.

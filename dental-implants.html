Understanding Implants

What Is a Dental Implant?

A dental implant is an artificial tooth-root system placed in the jawbone to support a replacement tooth.

Crown

The visible replacement tooth.

Abutment

Connects the crown to the implant.

Implant Fixture

Supports the implant in the bone.

Missing Teeth

Why Replace a Missing Tooth?

A missing tooth can affect everyday chewing and allow nearby teeth to shift.

Chewing — Helps restore everyday function.

Tooth Position — Helps maintain space.

Appearance — Replaces the visible tooth.

Tooth Replacement

Missing Tooth → Replacement Tooth

A missing tooth can leave a visible gap and affect everyday chewing. Tooth replacement can help restore the appearance and function of your smile.

Treatment Choices

What Are the Treatment Options?

Dental implants are one option for replacing a missing tooth. Other options may include bridges and removable dentures.

Dental Implant

A fixed replacement supported by an artificial root.

Dental Bridge

A replacement supported by neighbouring teeth.

Removable Denture

A removable option for replacing missing teeth.

Dental Implant Options

Dental Implant Options

Dental implants can replace one, several, or many missing teeth.

Selected Treatment

Single Tooth Implant

A single tooth implant is used to replace one missing tooth. An implant fixture supports a crown, helping restore the appearance and everyday function of the missing tooth.

Selected Treatment

Multiple Teeth Implants

Multiple teeth implants can be used when several teeth are missing. Implant-supported replacements can help restore chewing function and provide stable tooth replacement.

Selected Treatment

Full Mouth Implants

Full mouth implants may be considered when most or all teeth are missing or need replacement. Treatment is planned based on the condition of the mouth and the individual's needs.

Implant Component

What Is the Implant Screw?

The implant screw is the part placed inside the jawbone. It works like an artificial tooth root and helps support the replacement tooth.

Healing & Integration

What Is Osseointegration?

After placement, the implant goes through a healing period during which bone forms around and integrates with its surface.

Bone Support

What If There Isn't Enough Bone?

If there is not enough bone to support an implant, bone augmentation may sometimes be considered before implant placement. The appropriate option depends on the individual assessment.

Before You Choose

Things to Keep in Mind

Dental implants are not suitable for everyone. Treatment depends on factors such as bone support, gum health, oral hygiene and overall medical history.

A dentist can determine whether an implant is appropriate after an examination.

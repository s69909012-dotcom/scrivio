What are dental implants?

Dental implants are a tooth-replacement option for people with one or more missing teeth. A typical implant restoration can include an implant fixture, an abutment and a dental crown or another restoration. Whether implants are appropriate depends on the individual's dental condition and professional assessment.

UNDERSTANDING THE TREATMENT

Understanding Dental Implants

Dental implants are designed to replace the root portion of a missing tooth and support a restoration above the gumline.

01

Implant Fixture

An implant fixture is placed into the jawbone and can act as the foundation for a replacement tooth.

02

Abutment

The abutment connects the implant fixture with the dental restoration above the gumline.

03

Dental Crown

A crown or another suitable restoration can be attached to complete the tooth-replacement structure.

MISSING TOOTH REPLACEMENT

Dental Implants for Missing Teeth

Missing teeth can affect everyday activities such as eating and speaking. Dental implants are one tooth-replacement option that a dental professional may consider.

The appropriate approach can vary depending on the number and location of missing teeth, gum health, jawbone condition and other individual factors.

✓

Single missing tooth: An individual implant-supported restoration may be considered.

✓

Multiple missing teeth: Multiple implants or another tooth-replacement approach may be considered depending on the case.

✓

Full-mouth or full-arch situations: Implant-supported solutions may be considered after detailed professional evaluation.

i

Important to know

Not every person with missing teeth will be suitable for the same implant approach. A qualified dental professional needs to assess the mouth and determine the appropriate treatment plan.

TREATMENT PROCESS

What May Be Involved in Dental Implant Treatment?

Dental implant treatment is planned according to the individual's dental condition. The exact process can differ from person to person.

01

Assessment

The dental professional evaluates the teeth, gums, jawbone and overall oral condition.

02

Treatment Planning

The appropriate tooth-replacement approach is planned based on the individual's dental requirements.

03

Implant Placement

Where implants are considered suitable, the implant fixture may be placed into the jawbone.

04

Restoration

After the appropriate healing and planning stages, a suitable restoration may be attached.

BEFORE CONSIDERING TREATMENT

What to Keep in Mind About Dental Implants

Dental implant treatment is not identical for everyone. A proper professional evaluation is important before deciding whether an implant-supported solution is appropriate.

01

Oral health matters. The condition of the gums and remaining teeth can affect treatment planning.

02

Jawbone condition matters. The available bone may be evaluated before implant placement.

03

Treatment takes planning. Implant treatment can involve multiple stages and requires professional guidance.

04

Individual suitability varies. A dental professional should determine which treatment option is appropriate for your situation.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Dental Implant FAQs

Clear answers to common questions about dental implants and missing tooth replacement.

What are dental implants? +

Dental implants are a tooth-replacement option that uses an implant fixture placed in the jawbone, an abutment and a dental crown or other restoration. Suitability depends on an individual's oral health and professional evaluation.

Are dental implants suitable for a missing tooth? +

A dental implant may be considered for replacing a single missing tooth. However, the condition of the gums, jawbone, remaining teeth and overall oral health should be evaluated by a qualified dental professional.

What does dental implant treatment involve? +

Treatment may involve assessment, planning, implant placement and restoration. The exact sequence and timing depend on the individual's dental condition and treatment plan.

Can dental implants replace multiple missing teeth? +

Dental implants can be considered for multiple missing teeth and some full-mouth or full-arch tooth-replacement situations. The appropriate approach must be determined by a qualified dental professional.

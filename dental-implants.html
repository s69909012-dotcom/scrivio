A Simple Guide to Dental Implants

Learn what dental implants are, how they are placed, what recovery can involve, and what a dentist may consider before treatment.

Understanding Implants

What Is a Dental Implant?

A dental implant is a small post, often made from titanium, that is placed in the jawbone to support a replacement tooth.

The Three Main Parts

Implant Fixture

The part placed in the jawbone. It acts as the support for the replacement tooth.

Abutment

A connector that attaches to the implant and supports the replacement tooth.

Crown

The visible replacement tooth. Other types of replacement teeth may be used depending on the treatment.

Missing Teeth

What Can Happen After a Tooth Is Lost?

The effects of a missing tooth can vary from person to person. Tooth replacement is one option that may be discussed with a dentist.

Chewing — Losing a tooth can change how you chew or distribute pressure when eating.

Tooth Position — Nearby teeth may move or tilt into the space over time.

Appearance — A missing tooth can leave a visible gap, depending on its location.

Tooth Replacement

Missing Tooth → Replacement Tooth

Tooth replacement can involve an implant, bridge, denture, or another approach depending on the person's dental condition and treatment needs.

Treatment Choices

What Are the Treatment Options?

Dental implants are one way to replace missing teeth. Depending on the situation, a dental bridge or removable denture may also be considered.

Dental Implant

Uses an implant placed in the jawbone to support a replacement tooth or other dental restoration.

Dental Bridge

Uses neighbouring teeth or other dental support to hold one or more replacement teeth in place.

Removable Denture

A removable appliance used to replace some or many missing teeth.

Dental Implant Options

Dental Implant Options

The type of implant treatment depends on how many teeth are missing, the condition of the mouth, and the treatment plan developed by a dentist.

Selected Treatment

Single Tooth Implant

Used when one tooth is missing. An implant fixture can support an individual replacement tooth after the necessary healing and treatment steps.

Selected Treatment

Multiple Teeth Implants

May be considered when several teeth are missing. The number and arrangement of implants depends on the individual treatment plan.

Selected Treatment

Full Mouth Implants

May be considered when many or all teeth need replacement. The approach varies depending on the condition of the teeth, gums, jawbone and overall treatment needs.

Understanding the Option

What Makes Dental Implants Different?

Dental implants differ from bridges and removable dentures mainly in how the replacement is supported.

Jawbone Support

The implant fixture is placed in the jawbone.

Fixed Replacement

The replacement tooth is designed to stay in place rather than being removed like a denture.

Tooth Replacement

The treatment is intended to replace a missing tooth or teeth.

Visible Replacement

A crown or other restoration can replace the visible part of the missing tooth.

Implant Component

What Is the Implant Screw?

“Implant screw” is a common term patients use for the implant fixture. It is the part placed inside the jawbone and is designed to provide support for the replacement tooth.

Healing & Integration

How Does the Implant Heal With the Jawbone?

After placement, the jawbone needs time to heal around the implant. This process is called osseointegration — when bone grows around and bonds with the implant surface.

Healing takes time. The amount of time needed varies between patients and can depend on the implant location, bone condition, healing response and whether additional procedures are needed.

Treatment Journey

Dental Implant Treatment Process

The exact sequence and timing can vary from patient to patient. These are the main stages that may be involved.

01

Examination

The dentist examines your teeth, gums, bite and overall oral condition.

02

Imaging

Dental imaging may be used to assess the jawbone and nearby structures.

03

Planning

The treatment approach is planned according to your individual situation.

04

07

08

Ongoing cleaning and dental check-ups help monitor the implant and surrounding tissues.

During Implant Placement

What Happens During Implant Surgery?

Implant placement is commonly carried out as an outpatient procedure, although the setting and anaesthesia can vary depending on the patient and the planned treatment.

Anaesthesia

The treatment area is usually numbed with local anaesthesia. Other forms of sedation or anaesthesia may be considered in some situations.

Implant Placement

The dentist prepares the planned area of the jawbone and places the implant fixture in position.

After Placement

The area is allowed to heal. The next steps depend on the treatment plan and how healing progresses.

Recovery

What Is Recovery Like After Implant Surgery?

Recovery differs between patients. Some soreness, swelling or tenderness around the treated area can occur after surgery.

First Few Days

Mild to moderate discomfort, swelling or tenderness may occur. Your dental professional may give specific instructions about food, cleaning and medicines.

Eating & Cleaning

You may be advised to choose softer foods for a period and to clean the area carefully according to the instructions you receive.

Healing Period

The implant needs time to heal with the jawbone before later treatment steps are completed. The timing varies.

Follow-up

Follow-up appointments allow the dental professional to check healing and decide when the next treatment stage is appropriate.

When should you contact a dental professional?

Contact your treating dental professional if pain or swelling is getting worse rather than improving, bleeding is difficult to control, you develop fever or discharge, or you have another symptom that concerns you. Urgent symptoms should receive prompt professional attention.

During Healing

Can You Have a Temporary Tooth?

Sometimes. Depending on the location of the missing tooth and the treatment plan, a dentist may provide a temporary tooth or temporary replacement while the implant area heals. In other cases, the final replacement may need to wait until healing has progressed. Your dentist can explain what is appropriate for your situation.

Before Treatment

What Does a Dentist Check?

Implant suitability is based on an examination rather than on one factor alone.

Bone

The amount and condition of bone available for support.

Gums

Signs of gum disease or other gum problems.

Teeth

The condition of nearby and remaining teeth.

Bite

How your upper and lower teeth meet and share pressure.

Oral Hygiene

Whether the area can be kept clean during and after treatment.

Medical Factors

Health conditions, medicines and other factors that may affect healing.

Bone Support

What If There Isn't Enough Bone?

Some patients may have less bone available in the area where an implant is being considered.

Bone grafting is a procedure that adds bone material to an area where more bone may be needed for the planned treatment.

It is not required for every implant patient. If bone grafting is considered, the dentist can explain why it may be needed, what the procedure involves and whether additional healing time is required.

Risks & Considerations

What Are the Risks of Implant Surgery?

Dental implant surgery is a commonly performed procedure, but like other surgery, it can have risks. The level of risk varies from person to person and should be discussed with the treating dental professional.

Discomfort & Swelling

Soreness, swelling or tenderness can occur after the procedure and usually forms part of the early recovery period.

Bleeding or Infection

Bleeding and infection are possible with surgery. Following aftercare instructions can help support recovery.

Healing Problems

In some cases, the implant may not heal or integrate as expected and further treatment may be needed.

Nearby Structures

Depending on the implant location, nearby teeth, nerves or other structures may need to be considered during planning.

Long-Term Care

How Long Do Dental Implants Last?

There is no guaranteed number of years that an implant will last. Longevity varies between patients.

An implant can remain in use for many years when it heals well and is properly cared for, but problems can develop over time. The implant, surrounding gums and bone, and the replacement tooth all need ongoing care.

The crown or other replacement tooth may wear, become damaged or need replacement before the implant itself.

Gum Health Healthy tissues around the implant matter for long-term care.
Oral Hygiene Regular cleaning helps reduce problems around the implant.
Tobacco Use Smoking and tobacco use can affect healing and oral health.
Bite Forces Heavy or uneven forces can affect the implant and restoration.
Regular Check-ups Professional monitoring can help identify problems early.
Overall Factors Health, medicines and other individual factors may also matter.
Cost Information

What Can Affect the Cost of Dental Implants?

The cost of implant treatment can vary considerably because the treatment plan is different for each patient.

Number of Teeth

Replacing one tooth may involve different treatment steps from replacing several or many teeth.

Implant & Replacement Components

The implant system, abutment and type of replacement tooth can affect the overall treatment cost.

Additional Procedures

Some patients may need procedures such as tooth removal or bone grafting before or during implant treatment.

Follow-up & Maintenance

Follow-up visits, future repairs or replacement of the crown can also be part of the longer-term cost of care.

A dental professional can provide a more specific estimate after examining your mouth and understanding the treatment required.

Before You Choose

Things to Keep in Mind

A dentist considers several factors before deciding whether implant treatment is appropriate.

Bone Support

The dentist may assess whether there is enough suitable bone to support the planned implant.

Gum Health

Active gum disease may need to be treated before implant treatment is considered.

Smoking & Tobacco

Smoking and tobacco use can affect healing and the health of tissues around an implant, so it is important to discuss tobacco use openly.

Healing Conditions

Some medical conditions or medicines may affect healing. The dentist can consider your health history when planning treatment.

Oral Hygiene

Keeping the implant area and remaining teeth clean is an important part of long-term care.

Individual Treatment Plan

The number of implants, timing and additional procedures depend on the individual dental assessment.

After Treatment

Looking After an Implant

Follow Instructions — Follow the cleaning, eating and medicine instructions provided by your dental professional.

Keep the Area Clean — Good oral hygiene helps protect the implant and surrounding tissues.

Avoid Tobacco — Discuss smoking and tobacco use with your dentist, particularly around the time of treatment.

Attend Check-ups — Regular professional checks can monitor the implant, gums and replacement tooth.

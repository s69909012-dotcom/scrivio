What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are replacement tooth roots that are placed into the jawbone to support a dental crown, bridge or denture. They can be an option for people with one or more missing teeth, depending on their oral health, bone condition and individual treatment needs.

What Are Dental Implants?

A dental implant is a tooth replacement system designed to replace the root of a missing tooth. The implant is placed in the jawbone and can support a dental restoration such as a crown.

Dental implant treatment is planned individually. A dental professional may assess the teeth, gums, jawbone and other factors before determining whether implants are an appropriate option.

Who May Be Considered for Dental Implants?

Dental implants may be considered for people who have lost one or more teeth. Suitability depends on an individual's oral health and treatment requirements.

  • People with a missing single tooth
  • People with multiple missing teeth
  • People requiring a tooth replacement option
  • People with suitable oral and bone conditions

A dental assessment is necessary to determine whether implant treatment is appropriate.

How Does the Dental Implant Procedure Work?

Dental implant treatment generally involves several stages. The exact sequence and duration can vary depending on the patient's individual treatment plan.

1

Initial Assessment

The dental professional evaluates oral health and the condition of the teeth, gums and jawbone.

2

Treatment Planning

A personalized treatment approach is planned according to the patient's dental needs.

3

Implant Placement

The implant fixture is placed into the jawbone as part of the planned treatment.

4

Healing

The implant is allowed to heal and integrate with the surrounding bone.

5

Restoration

A suitable dental restoration, such as a crown, may be attached after the appropriate healing stage.

What Are the Main Parts of a Dental Implant?

A dental implant restoration commonly consists of three main components.

  • Implant fixture: The component placed into the jawbone.
  • Abutment: The connector between the implant and restoration.
  • Crown: The visible tooth-shaped restoration.

What Are the Benefits of Dental Implants?

Dental implants are one option for replacing missing teeth. Potential benefits depend on the individual's treatment plan.

Tooth Replacement

Implants can be used as part of a treatment plan to replace missing teeth.

Natural-Looking Restoration

A dental crown can be designed to resemble the appearance of a natural tooth.

Functional Restoration

Tooth replacement may help restore everyday oral function, depending on the individual case.

Individualized Treatment

Implant treatment can be planned according to the number and location of missing teeth and other clinical factors.

Dental Implants vs Other Tooth Replacement Options

Different tooth replacement options are used in different situations. A dental professional can help determine which options may be appropriate.

Option Common Use Main Concept
Dental Implant Missing tooth or teeth Uses an implant fixture to support a restoration
Dental Bridge Replacing a missing tooth or teeth Uses supporting teeth for the restoration
Denture Replacing multiple or many missing teeth Removable tooth replacement option

How Long Does Dental Implant Treatment Take?

Dental implant treatment does not have the same timeline for every patient. The overall duration can depend on the condition of the jawbone, gum health, healing, number of implants and whether additional procedures are required.

How Much Do Dental Implants Cost?

Dental implant costs can vary depending on the number of implants, type of restoration, implant system, additional procedures, treatment complexity and clinic location.

An individual treatment assessment is needed for an accurate treatment plan and cost estimate.

What Should You Consider Before Dental Implant Treatment?

Implant treatment requires individual assessment. Several factors may influence treatment planning.

Gum Health

The condition of the gums can be an important consideration when planning implant treatment.

Jawbone Condition

The available bone can influence implant treatment planning and whether additional procedures may be required.

Overall Oral Health

Existing dental conditions may need to be assessed before implant treatment.

Individual Treatment Plan

The appropriate treatment approach varies from person to person and should be determined by a qualified dental professional.

Dental Implant FAQs

Answers to common questions about dental implants and the treatment process.

What are dental implants?

Dental implants are replacement tooth roots placed into the jawbone to support a dental restoration such as a crown, bridge or denture.

Who can get dental implants?

Dental implants may be considered for people with missing teeth, but suitability depends on individual oral health, jawbone condition and other treatment factors.

How does the dental implant procedure work?

Treatment commonly involves assessment, treatment planning, implant placement, a healing period and placement of the final restoration.

How long does dental implant treatment take?

Treatment duration varies between patients. Healing, bone condition, gum health, number of implants and additional procedures can affect the overall timeline.

How much do dental implants cost?

Dental implant costs vary depending on the treatment plan, number of implants, restoration type, implant system, additional procedures and location.

Are dental implants suitable for everyone?

Not necessarily. A dental professional needs to assess oral health, gums, jawbone and other factors before determining suitability.

What is the difference between a dental implant and a dental bridge?

A dental implant uses an implant fixture placed in the jawbone to support a restoration, while a traditional dental bridge uses supporting teeth to hold the replacement tooth or teeth.
